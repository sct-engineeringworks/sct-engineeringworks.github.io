## The challenge

A national reference form-measurement machine must do more than move and acquire data. Its mechanical architecture must make nanometre-scale performance credible by controlling geometry, positioning, stability, interfaces, error sources, and the path from calibration to analysis.

The project brought together mechanics, controls, instrumentation, metrology, algorithms, suppliers, and commissioning over a multi-year development cycle.

## My contribution

I led the mechanical workstream and coordinated a 6-person cross-functional team using Kanban and structured design reviews. My work included:

- developing the ultra-precision mechanical architecture;
- integrating micrometric positioning axes;
- performing tolerance analyses and design reviews;
- addressing technical risks affecting stability and repeatability;
- supporting measurement protocols and analysis algorithms;
- coordinating precision components, metrology equipment, suppliers, build, and commissioning;
- structuring documentation for maintainability and institutional reuse.

## Engineering approach

### Connect architecture to uncertainty

Mechanical choices were considered through their influence on measurement behaviour. Interfaces, alignment, positioning, thermal sensitivity, repeatability, and calibration were treated as a connected system.

### Develop by verifiable increments

The workstream used visible priorities, cross-disciplinary reviews, and incremental validation. This allowed technical dependencies to be exposed early and decisions to be connected to testable outcomes.

### Preserve traceability beyond the prototype

ISO-aligned measurement protocols, structured technical documentation, and analysis contributions supported repeatability, maintainability, and continued use within the institute.

## What this demonstrates

The project demonstrates long-horizon technical delivery where mechanical design, measurement science, risk control, supplier coordination, validation, and documentation must remain aligned.

> At ultra-precision scale, the machine architecture, the measurement model, and the validation plan cannot be designed independently.
