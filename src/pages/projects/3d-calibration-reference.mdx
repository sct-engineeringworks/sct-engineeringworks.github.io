## The challenge

Full-field optical measurement requires a physical reference that presents known, repeatable geometry to the sensor across the useful measurement field. The solution had to support consistent calibration while remaining practical to fabricate, handle, verify, document, and deploy in production.

## My contribution

I designed and industrialized a three-dimensional calibration reference, described professionally as an artificial head. The public scope of the contribution includes:

- translating the calibration objective into functional and geometric requirements;
- developing the physical reference architecture and detailed design;
- supporting fabrication, verification, and iteration;
- defining how the reference would be used within the calibration process;
- documenting the solution for repeatable use and transfer;
- coordinating adoption across two production sites.

## Engineering approach

### Treat the artefact as part of the calibration model

The value of a calibration reference comes from the relationship between its geometry, traceability, positioning, sensor field, procedure, and acceptance evidence. These elements were considered together.

### Design for repeated operational use

Industrialization required more than demonstrating a concept. The reference needed a stable definition, practical handling, reproducible use, verification logic, and documentation suitable for more than one site.

### Standardize without hiding uncertainty

Standardization was used to improve consistency while preserving attention to setup, repeatability, calibration conditions, and the limits of the reference.

## Outcomes

| Outcome | Public evidence |

## What this demonstrates

This project demonstrates the translation of a measurement need into a physical product that can move from concept and prototyping to verified, documented use in production.

> A useful calibration artefact is not simply an accurate shape. It is a controlled interface between geometry, procedure, measurement, and operations.
