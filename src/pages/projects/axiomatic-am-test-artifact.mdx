## The engineering question

No single benchmark geometry can represent every additive-manufacturing process, machine, scale, material, and inspection constraint. The project therefore addressed a more durable question:

> How can a test artifact be designed systematically for a defined set of geometric-performance and measurement needs?

I developed a requirements-led method that decomposed the evaluation objective, grouped related functions into modules, selected measurable geometric features, and iterated the layout against manufacturing, inspection, material, and build-time constraints.

## Three inseparable project axes

| Axis | Question addressed | Demonstrated work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mechanical and axiomatic design** | What geometry should be created, and why? | Functional decomposition, requirement modules, FR–DP reasoning, feature selection, orientation, compact layout, CAD, dimensions, and GD&T intent |
| **Precision metrology** | How will the design intent be measured and interpreted? | Datum strategy, scanner and probe access, laser point-cloud comparison, tactile CMM inspection, and physical form, size, and orientation deviations |
| **Technical project delivery** | How can the work be scoped, executed, verified, and communicated? | Literature review, priorities, deliberate exclusions, resource constraints, staged design–build–measure delivery, evidence control, and publication milestones |

## Axis 1 — Mechanical and axiomatic design

The historical definition contained **13 functional requirements**, **5 design constraints**, and **14 feature groups**. The resulting artifact combined planar, prismatic, cylindrical, conical, spherical, internal, external, coaxial, slot, chamfer, and free-form features within a reported envelope of **73.8 × 68.85 × 16.65 mm**.

The method did not begin with a predetermined geometry. It began with the evaluation purpose, then followed a controlled sequence:

1. define and classify the requirements;
2. decompose each need into elementary functions;
3. group related functions to limit redundant features;
4. select geometry, size, orientation, and repetition from the intended measurands;
5. preserve scanner or probe access;
6. balance coverage against material, build time, post-processing, and inspection effort.

The research was later formalized with Axiomatic Design concepts: functional requirements, design parameters, design-matrix reasoning, requirement independence, and information-conscious solution selection.

## Axis 2 — Precision metrology

The artifact was physically manufactured and inspected. The available record documents the following chain:

| Item | Recorded condition |
| --- | --- |
| Process and machine | Selective Laser Melting on an AddUp FormUp 350 |
| Material | NC718, reported as Inconel alloy 718 |
| Layer thickness | 40 µm |
| Laser power | 190 W contouring; 201 W hatching |
| Build plate | Not preheated |
| Build duration | Approximately 8 hours |
| Inspection condition | Artifact retained on the build plate |
| Global comparison | Kreon laser scanner on a CMM; point-cloud-to-CAD comparison in CATIA and verification in GOM Inspect |
| Form and orientation | Contact CMM with Renishaw MODUS |

The numerical record contains **39 form and dimensional values** plus **23 orientation values**. Selected generated summaries are shown below.

| Physical observation | Reported result | Engineering reading |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Flatness of four base corner planes | Mean **0.367 mm**; range **0.2277–0.4682 mm** | Large lower-artifact deviations were associated with an apparent angular build-plate offset |
| Form deviation of X/Y-oriented cylinders | Mean **0.595 mm** | Cross-build cylinders were markedly less accurate than Z-oriented cylindrical surfaces |
| Form deviation of Z cylindrical surfaces | Mean **0.081 mm** | Orientation strongly influenced form accuracy |
| Form deviation of unsupported cones | Mean **0.587 mm** | Visual and measured evidence both supported a support-aware design strategy |
| External-feature dimensional deviation | Mean **0.041 mm** | External Z-oriented features were the more accurate comparison group |
| Internal-feature dimensional deviation | Mean **0.173 mm** | The mean was **4.24×** the external-feature value |
| Maximum reported concentricity error | **0.928 mm** | Coaxial relationships exposed a major alignment deviation |

![Generated summary of the reported physical metrology results](/projects/am-test-artifact/metrology-summary.svg)

The experiment was diagnostic rather than statistical: one compact artifact made orientation effects, unsupported-feature defects, internal-feature difficulty, and the apparent build-plate offset visible in a connected inspection problem.

## Axis 3 — Technical project delivery

The work was managed as a sequence of engineering decisions rather than as an isolated CAD exercise.

| Phase | Management decision | Principal output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research framing | Compare existing artifacts and replace geometry-first selection with a requirements-led method | State-of-the-art review and design criteria |
| Scope and priorities | Focus the first experiment on general GD&T assessment; defer causal experiments, repeat builds, and assembly pairs | Ranked requirements and explicit exclusions |
| Concept development | Group related requirements to reduce geometric redundancy and resource consumption | Requirement modules and feature families |
| Embodiment design | Balance coverage, accessibility, buildability, envelope, material, and post-processing | CAD model, feature architecture, and dimensioned drawing |
| Build and inspection planning | Fix material, process parameters, on-plate inspection, laser scanning, and tactile checks | Manufacturing and inspection plan |
| Verification and learning | Compare the physical artifact with nominal geometry and identify dominant deviations | Measurement tables, deviation maps, conclusions, and next actions |
| Dissemination and reuse | Transform the study into durable research and engineering evidence | 2019 conference paper, 2020 journal article, and controlled portfolio case study |

## What this project demonstrates

- **Design:** the ability to translate broad evaluation objectives into a compact, measurable mechanical architecture.
- **Metrology:** the ability to connect feature intent, access, datums, inspection technology, physical results, and interpretation limits.
- **Project delivery:** the ability to control scope, resources, interfaces, evidence, decisions, and dissemination across a complete engineering lifecycle.

## Evidence boundary

The values describe **one manufactured artifact**. The available archive does not include the raw point cloud, native CMM report, calibration records, repeat builds, or a measurement-uncertainty budget. The reported deviations combine effects from CAD-to-STL conversion, manufacture, alignment, measurement, filtering, and data treatment. They characterize the observed artifact-and-inspection chain; they do not establish machine capability.
